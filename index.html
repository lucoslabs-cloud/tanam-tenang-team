What the platform does

TanamTenang is a farm-adaptation decision-support tool for Sri Aman district, Sarawak. It scores every 250 m grid cell in the district for agricultural suitability — today and under six future climate scenarios — and helps farmers choose what to grow and how to rotate crops. It is built entirely from open, public datasets; every number on screen names its source.

Likely Q&A — everyone should know

What is the baseline "Today"?
The 1991–2020 climate normal from NASA POWER. Thirty years of observed daily weather, averaged into monthly statistics for each metric.
Why Sri Aman specifically?
It was chosen as the pilot district because it represents a mix of lowland agriculture (wet paddy, oil palm), peat soil challenges, and smallholder farming common across Sarawak's interior. The same pipeline can be re-run for any district.
Is the data free and public?
Yes. Every dataset used is publicly available from its source agency (NASA, ISRIC, OCHA). No licensing fees, no proprietary data, no field surveys.

The grid

The district is divided into 67,855 cells at 250 m resolution, matching SoilGrids 2.0's native resolution — the coarsest scored dataset that varies meaningfully at that scale. Each cell is one row in the grid_cells database table. The boundary is from OCHA COD-AB (the UN's Common Operational Datasets – Administrative Boundaries for Sri Aman).

Climate values are not stored per cell. They come from the nearest NASA POWER point at query time, which is why the same cell shows different climate numbers under different scenarios without re-ingesting.

Erosion Exposure — how it's computed

RUSLE without cover and practice factors: what rain, soil, and slope would do to bare ground. Three components:

R — Rainfall erosivity
From NASA POWER precipitation data
K — Soil erodibility
From SoilGrids texture and organic carbon
LS — Slope length-steepness
From SRTM (slope_length assumed 50 m; no field boundaries in the data)

Result scaled 0–100 against the 95th percentile of the district. Important: it is not tonnes per hectare — the cover factor (what you grow) is deliberately excluded because that is what the farmer decides.

What SoilGrids actually is

SoilGrids 2.0 is not a field survey. It is a machine learning model (random forests) trained on ~240,000 soil profiles from ISRIC's global database, with remote sensing and terrain data as predictors. The 250 m output is a spatial prediction, not a measurement. Each value comes with an uncertainty interval, though the platform uses the median prediction. This is worth knowing if someone asks how "accurate" the soil data is — it is as accurate as the ML model, which is high at the continental scale but smooths over local variation.

Q&A — Geospatial

Why 250 m cells and not 30 m (SRTM's resolution)?
SoilGrids and MODIS are the coarsest scored datasets at 250 m. Using 30 m cells would imply soil data precision that the data doesn't have. The resolution was chosen to match the most limiting dataset, not the finest one.
Where does distance to water come from — is it from OpenStreetMap?
No. It is derived from the SRTM DEM's own flow accumulation — any cell that enough land drains into is counted as a watercourse. OSM supplies the base map tiles and nothing that is scored. This means unmapped drains still count, but dry gullies may also count.
Is pond water temperature measured by satellite?
No — there is no satellite product for a pond that doesn't exist yet. It is modelled: mean air temperature (POWER), corrected for elevation lapse rate (SRTM: 6.5°C per km), plus ~1°C for a shallow tropical pond. It moves with the climate scenario because it is derived at query time, not stored.

ET₀ — how it's calculated

Method: FAO-56 Penman-Monteith. This is the UN Food and Agriculture Organization's standard reference evapotranspiration formula — the same formula used globally for irrigation scheduling.

Inputs from POWER: temperature (min/max), solar radiation, relative humidity, wind speed.

Under future scenarios: Relative humidity is held constant (standard assumption for the humid tropics — air holds more water as it warms). The dew point is recomputed so RH stays unchanged. ET₀ then rises through the growing vapour pressure deficit. A Hargreaves cross-check (temperature-only formula) is computed independently as a sanity check. At 2070 SSP5-8.5: ET₀ rises from 1,209 to 1,286 mm (+6.4%).

Delta change method — the key idea

CMIP6 models are biased for Borneo by 1–3°C in absolute temperature and hundreds of mm in rainfall. If we used their raw numbers, the model's bias (not climate change) would decide what grows.

Instead, we use only the change: future minus past, within the same model. Systematic biases cancel in that subtraction.

Temperature:  projected = observed + (model_future − model_past)   [additive]
Rainfall:     projected = observed × (model_future / model_past)   [multiplicative]

Both computed month by month (12 change factors per model per variable). Applied day by day to all 45 years of observed weather. The result: a 45-year synthetic daily record for each future scenario.

Why daily, not monthly means? Dry-spell length, heat-stress days, and extreme rainfall are tail statistics — invisible in a monthly mean. They can only be computed from a daily sequence.

Important caveats to know

Wind, humidity and radiation are held at today's values. The projections change temperature and rainfall only. Wind and radiation are the quantities CMIP6 models simulate least well, so they were not fetched.

The model spread is not a confidence interval. Six models are six opinions. The range says how far apart they are, not the probability of a given outcome.

Heat-stress days stay at zero in every scenario. Daily maxima in equatorial Borneo do not reach 35°C. This is correct. The THI (temperature-humidity index) does move — from 82.8 to 86.6 under SSP5-8.5 2070 — which matters for livestock and pond aquaculture.

Dry spell lengths barely change despite daily computation. The 90th-percentile longest dry spell is 13 days in every scenario. The worst droughts are genuinely rainless — multiplying by a small factor doesn't interrupt them. This is a real finding, not a bug.

Q&A — Climate & Met

What is SSP and why are there two?
SSP = Shared Socioeconomic Pathway. It is the IPCC's framework for linking emissions trajectories to socioeconomic assumptions. SSP2-4.5 is roughly current policy (medium mitigation); SSP5-8.5 is a fossil-fuelled high-growth scenario — not the "worst case" but the high end for stress-testing. We use both so farmers can see the range of possible futures.
How did you access the CMIP6 data?
Via OPeNDAP through NASA THREDDS. The files are global NetCDFs (one year per file, one variable per file, ~15 GB each globally). We fetched only a 24-cell bounding box over Sri Aman using an ASCII subset query — about 130 kB instead of 15 GB. The whole district costs the same as a single cell because CMIP6 files are chunked by time, not space.
Why not just use monthly climate projections?
Because the most dangerous climate signals are in the tails, not the means. Monthly averages hide dry-spell length, heat-stress days, and rainfall extremes — the things that actually kill crops. Applying change factors to daily data and recomputing everything from scratch is the only way to capture them.

FAO land evaluation — the framework

The platform adapts the FAO Framework for Land Evaluation (1976, still the global standard). Each crop has a set of requirements — optimal and absolute ranges for each environmental metric. Each metric is scored 0 to 1 based on how close the site's conditions are to optimal. The scores are combined into a single 0–100 figure. The class (S1/S2/S3/N1/N2) is assigned by where that figure falls.

Key principle — Liebig's Law of the Minimum: The limiting factor governs, not the average. Good rainfall cannot compensate for wrong soil pH. This is why the platform uses a weighted geometric mean, not an arithmetic mean.

How the suitability score is calculated

Method: Weighted geometric mean (computed in SQL)

score = EXP( SUM(weight × LN(MAX(metric_score, 0.02))) / SUM(weight) )

Floor of 0.02: Prevents one zero from destroying all ranking information. A cell with one completely failing metric still gets ranked, though it will score very low.

25% minimum-factor pull: After the geometric mean, the single worst metric pulls the result 25% further down toward itself. Sharpens the "weakest link" signal.

Group weights: Climate = 1.0, Terrain = 1.0, Soil = 1.0, Water = 1.0, Vegetation = 0.5. Vegetation gets half weight because NDVI records what is already growing — rewarding existing vegetation would disadvantage bare farmland.

Rotation rules — disease & pest basis

How rotation rules work: Each crop has a plant family. The planner checks the gap since the same family last appeared. Within the same family:

  • Fabaceae after Fabaceae: Harmful — reduces nitrogen fixation benefit, risk of Rhizobium imbalance
  • Cucurbitaceae after Cucurbitaceae: Fusarium crown and root rot risk
  • Albugo (white rust) after kangkung (Convolvulaceae)
  • Scoring: 0 gaps (same family repeated) = 0.0, 1 season gap = 0.33, 2 seasons = 0.67, ≥3 seasons = 1.0 (full break)

Legume cap: More than half the rotation in legumes is not better — it has its own disease risk. The legume index caps at 1.0 when half the seasons are legumes.

Key finding — wet paddy & climate scenarios

★
Wet paddy scores 1.00 on all climate metrics in every scenario. Its temperature, rainfall, and other climate inputs all sit inside the optimal range both today and in 2070 under high emissions. The score doesn't change — not because climate change doesn't matter, but because wet paddy's limits here are slope, flood hazard, and soil pH, none of which climate affects. Liebig's Law: the limiting factor governs, and the limiting factors aren't climate.

Q&A — Agriculture

How did you decide the suitability envelopes for each crop?
From published agronomic literature — FAO crop guidelines, IRRI rice production manuals, Sarawak DOA crop management guides, and peer-reviewed journal thresholds. The envelopes encode the conditions each crop has been found to need, not modelled relationships.
Why is NDVI not used in the yield priority?
Deliberately excluded. NDVI records what is growing now. Scoring it would reward existing forest and penalise a paddy field photographed between seasons — recommending land because something else already has it. NDVI is shown as evidence in the site report, but land cover (not greenness) is what blocks unsuitable land use.
How does disease factor into the suitability score?
Relative humidity and temperature together signal fungal disease pressure. Each crop's suitability envelope sets thresholds for these — a site that is persistently humid beyond the crop's tolerance gets a lower score on those metrics. The rotation planner adds a separate disease signal through the pest-break index (plant family rotation rules).
What does "put forward" mean on a rotation strategy?
"Put forward" is the strategy the platform recommends based on the farmer's stated priorities. It is not "best" in an absolute sense — it is the best match for what the farmer said they care about. Its gaps and risks sections are open by default because a farmer who reads only the recommendation should still see its limits.

Database architecture

grid_cells
67,855 rows. One per 250m cell. Holds all terrain, soil, water, and vegetation columns (fixed — same regardless of scenario).
climate_normals
Holds the scenario-variable climate data: monthly normals per POWER grid point, per scenario. The scoring engine joins this to grid_cells at query time.
enterprise_requirements
One row per crop × metric. Defines the trapezoidal scoring envelope for each metric for each crop.
climate_scenarios
CMIP6 delta factors per model, per month, per variable. Used by build-scenarios to produce the rows in climate_normals.

Water temperature: Not stored as a column — it is derived at query time from POWER air temperature, elevation (SRTM lapse rate), and a +1°C pond offset. This is why it follows the climate scenario automatically.

Scoring engine — the core formula

Built as SQL, executed in PostgreSQL. The weighted geometric mean:

EXP(
  SUM(requirement_weight * LN(GREATEST(metric_score, 0.02)))
  /
  SUM(requirement_weight)
)

When priority sliders are applied, the engine adjusts each metric's weight before this formula:

new_weight = old_weight × (1 + (multiplier − 1) × slider_value)

Then all weights are normalised, so turning everything up is equivalent to turning nothing up. The normalisation happens automatically in the geometric mean's division by SUM(weight).

Scenario integrity — ScenarioCatalogue

Before the UI offers a scenario, ScenarioCatalogue checks that the climate_normals table has a complete set of 12 monthly normals at every POWER sampling point for that scenario. A scenario that failed mid-build (network error, partial ingest) would have silent holes — cells near one sampling point using baseline data, cells near another using future data — and the map would look plausible while being wrong. The catalogue prevents this by either showing a scenario completely or not at all. The tile endpoint also refuses a scenario the catalogue has not verified.

Hackathon static build (hackathon/)

A zero-install version of the platform. No server, no database — all 67,855 cells' data is embedded as compressed JSON inside the Vite bundle. Opens in any browser by double-clicking index.html.

It mirrors the main platform's scoring engine in TypeScript: the same weighted geometric mean formula, the same FAO classes, the same priority slider logic. The two can drift if config changes, so the hackathon build is a snapshot of the platform at submission time.

Important: The hackathon directory contains a nested git repository (the full platform clone used to mirror the engine). This is excluded from the main repo's tracking via .gitignore.

Q&A — IT

Why is the scoring done in SQL rather than application code?
Because the scoring is a sum over 44 metrics for 67,855 cells — that's 2.98 million multiplications. Doing this in a loop in PHP would be slow. PostgreSQL executes the weighted geometric mean as a single aggregation query across the full table, which is much faster. It also keeps the logic close to the data, which makes it auditable.
How does changing a priority slider affect the map?
The slider value (0–1) is multiplied into each metric's weight via the formula: new_weight = old_weight × (1 + (multiplier − 1) × slider). The multipliers are defined per metric per priority in config (e.g. flood hazard gets ×2.0 for risk aversion). Then the scoring SQL re-runs with the adjusted weights. The geometry mean's normalization means turning all sliders up is the same as turning none up.
What stops a future scenario from silently using baseline data?
ScenarioCatalogue. It queries the database before returning the scenario list and excludes any scenario that doesn't have a complete set of monthly normals across all sampling points. The tile-rendering endpoint also rejects an unverified scenario ID rather than falling through to baseline rows.

Terms & Definitions

AWC — Available Water Capacity Soil
The volume of water (mm) the soil can store in the root zone that plants can actually access — above wilting point, below field capacity. Derived from SoilGrids texture, organic carbon, and bulk density. The soil's buffer against a dry spell.
Baseline Climate
The 1991–2020 climate normal from NASA POWER. Thirty years of observed daily weather, averaged into monthly statistics for each metric. Called "Today" in the platform interface.
Bulk Density Soil
How compacted the soil is, in kg/dm³. High values mean roots struggle to penetrate and water ponds on the surface. From SoilGrids. Also used to derive drainage class and AWC.
CEC — Cation Exchange Capacity Soil
The soil's ability to hold positively charged nutrient ions (calcium, magnesium, potassium) instead of letting them leach away with rainfall. Measured in cmol(+)/kg. From SoilGrids. High CEC = more fertile, lower fertiliser requirements.
CMIP6 Climate
Coupled Model Intercomparison Project Phase 6. The latest coordinated set of global climate model runs used in the IPCC's Sixth Assessment Report (AR6). The platform uses six models from CMIP6 to produce future scenarios.
Delta Change Method Climate
A method for constructing future climate scenarios. Instead of using CMIP6 model outputs directly (which are biased for Borneo), only the change between the model's future and its own past is used. That change is then applied to the observed weather record. Temperature: additive. Rainfall: multiplicative. Applied month by month, day by day.
Drainage Class Soil
How readily water leaves the soil profile, coded 1 (very poorly drained) to 7 (excessively drained). Derived from SoilGrids texture, bulk density, and organic carbon. Very poorly drained soils suit wet paddy but not vegetables; excessively drained soils need irrigation.
ECS — Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity Climate
How much the global mean temperature would eventually increase for a doubling of atmospheric CO₂. Used to characterise the "strength" of a climate model's warming response. The six models in the platform span ECS from 1.9 to 5.3°C, covering the full credible range.
ET₀ — Reference Evapotranspiration Climate
The amount of water a reference grass crop would lose to the atmosphere in a year. Derived using the FAO-56 Penman-Monteith formula from POWER temperature, solar radiation, humidity, and wind. Represents the demand side of the water budget.
FAO Land Evaluation Agriculture
Framework for Land Evaluation (FAO, 1976). The methodology behind the platform's suitability classes. Defines how to match land characteristics to crop requirements, and how to assign suitability classes (S1/S2/S3/N1/N2) based on the degree of match.
Flood Hazard Terrain
A 0–1 composite index of flood exposure, derived from HAND (height above drainage), TWI (topographic wetness), and slope. 0 = safe; 1 = floods regularly. This metric gets a ×2.0 multiplier under the "Avoid total loss" priority — the highest multiplier in the platform.
HAND — Height Above Nearest Drainage Terrain
The vertical distance (m) from each cell to the nearest stream or drainage channel, as determined by routing water downhill through the SRTM DEM. Cells with low HAND flood first and drain last. Distinct from elevation above sea level.
Liebig's Law of the Minimum Agriculture
The principle that growth is controlled by the most limiting factor, not the average of all factors. A plant cannot grow on the average of good rainfall and wrong soil pH. This is why the platform uses a weighted geometric mean: one bad metric drags the whole score down. Named after Justus von Liebig (1840).
MERRA-2 Climate
Modern-Era Retrospective Analysis for Research and Applications, Version 2. NASA's atmospheric reanalysis product — a model run that assimilates decades of observations into a coherent gridded dataset. The meteorological backbone of NASA POWER.
MODIS Satellite
Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer. NASA instrument on the Terra and Aqua satellites. The platform uses three MODIS products: MOD13Q1 (250 m NDVI), MCD12Q1 (500 m land cover), and MOD11A2 (1 km daytime land surface temperature).
NDVI — Normalised Difference Vegetation Index Satellite
A measure of vegetation greenness from MODIS (MOD13Q1), ranging −1 to +1. The platform uses mean NDVI and NDVI amplitude (seasonal swing). It is shown as evidence but deliberately excluded from the yield priority multipliers — NDVI records what grows now, not what could grow.
NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 Climate
NASA Earth Exchange Global Daily Downscaled Projections, CMIP6 edition. Daily, 0.25° (~25 km), bias-corrected climate model outputs for all CMIP6 scenarios. Served by NASA NCCS over THREDDS/OPeNDAP. The source of all six future scenarios in the platform.
OPeNDAP IT
Open-source Protocol for Data Access and Population. A protocol for requesting subsets of large scientific datasets over the web without downloading the whole file. The platform uses it to fetch a 24-cell bounding box (~130 kB) from NEX-GDDP files that are ~15 GB globally.
Peat Depth Soil
Depth (cm) of peat in the soil. Deep peat (>50 cm) subsides and acidifies when drained, releases large amounts of CO₂, and is a major carbon store. Important for Sri Aman, which has areas of peat swamp forest. From SoilGrids.
Pest-Break Index Agriculture
A 0–1 score measuring how well a rotation disrupts pest and disease cycles. Computed from the gap between crops of the same plant family. 0 = same family in consecutive seasons; 1 = three or more seasons since the same family. Averaged across all seasons in the rotation.
NASA POWER Climate
Prediction Of Worldwide Energy Resources. NASA's publicly available daily climate dataset combining MERRA-2 meteorological reanalysis and CERES satellite solar radiation. Covers 45+ years, ~55×69 km grid. The source of all 17 climate metrics in the platform. Free REST API, no authentication.
RUSLE Terrain
Revised Universal Soil Loss Equation. The formula for estimating erosion risk from rainfall erosivity (R), soil erodibility (K), slope length-steepness (LS), cover (C), and practice (P). The platform computes R×K×LS only — C and P are what the farmer decides, so they are excluded.
ScenarioCatalogue IT
A Laravel class that checks whether each climate scenario has complete monthly normals across all sampling points before allowing the UI to offer it. Prevents a half-built scenario from silently using baseline data for some cells. A scenario is either complete or absent — no partial states.
SMAP L4 Satellite
Soil Moisture Active Passive Level 4, 9 km resolution. NASA satellite product combining microwave soil moisture observations with a land surface model. The source of mean soil moisture and dry-season soil moisture in the platform.
SoilGrids 2.0 Soil
ISRIC's global soil property predictions at 250 m, generated by random forest ML models trained on ~240,000 soil profiles with remote sensing and terrain predictors. The source of all 12 soil metrics. Each output is a probabilistic prediction, not a measurement.
SOC — Soil Organic Carbon Soil
Organic carbon content of the topsoil, measured in g/kg. The core measure of soil health, structure, and water-holding capacity. Gets the highest multiplier (×1.9) under the "Build soil health" priority. From SoilGrids.
SSP — Shared Socioeconomic Pathway Climate
IPCC framework linking future emissions to socioeconomic development storylines. SSP2-4.5 = "Middle of the road" (roughly current policy, moderate mitigation). SSP5-8.5 = "Fossil-fuelled development" (high emissions; not worst case but high end). The number after the hyphen (4.5, 8.5) is the radiative forcing in W/m² by 2100.
SRTM — Shuttle Radar Topography Mission Terrain
30 m (1 arc-second) digital elevation model from a February 2000 Space Shuttle mission. The source of all terrain metrics: slope, aspect, TWI, HAND, flood hazard, erosion exposure (LS component), and distance to water. Free via NASA EarthData.
THI — Temperature-Humidity Index Climate
A combined measure of thermal stress in humid conditions. In humid air, evaporative cooling is impaired — animals stop gaining weight and ponds lose dissolved oxygen long before the thermometer looks alarming. Heat-stress days (°C threshold) stay at zero in Borneo, but THI max does rise under future scenarios (82.8 → 86.6 under SSP5-8.5 2070).
TWI — Topographic Wetness Index Terrain
ln(flow accumulation / tan(slope)). A terrain index indicating how strongly the shape of the land concentrates water at each cell. High TWI cells stay waterlogged and are prone to saturation. Derived from SRTM flow accumulation.
Water Balance (Annual) Climate
Annual rainfall minus annual ET₀. Negative values mean the site runs a water deficit — evaporation demands more than falls as rain — and irrigation or water storage is needed. Important for dryland crops in drier parts of the district.
Weighted Geometric Mean IT
The aggregation method used to combine individual metric scores into one overall suitability score. Unlike an arithmetic mean, one low score drags the entire result down — encoding Liebig's Law. Formula: exp(Σ(weight × ln(score)) / Σ(weight)). Computed in SQL.
